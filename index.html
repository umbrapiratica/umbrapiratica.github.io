Operating principles

Cybersecurity research only creates value when scope, evidence, accountability, and authorization are treated as first-class product requirements.

Explicit authorization

Assessment and validation work is limited to systems that are owned by, operated by, or explicitly authorized for testing by the relevant organization.

Least necessary impact

Prefer passive and non-destructive validation whenever it can answer the security question with sufficient confidence.

Human approval for material risk

Higher-impact actions should be bound to a specific target, purpose, scope, credential context, expected result, and stop condition.

Evidence before conclusions

Claims should be traceable to observations, preserved artifacts, and repeatable checks so remediation can be verified rather than assumed.
